The discipline

1. Conceptual, logical and physical models

A data model is described at three levels. Each answers a different question for a different audience, and an AI draft often skips straight to the physical level. Ask for all three: disagreements at the conceptual level are cheap to fix, and expensive once they are tables.

LevelQuestion it answersAudienceContainsDoes not contain
ConceptualWhat things does the business care about, and how do they relate?Executives, product ownersEntities and named relationships (a Party holds a Policy)Attributes, keys, data types
LogicalWhat do we need to know about each thing, and what identifies it?Analysts, architects, stewardsAttributes, identifiers, cardinality, normalization to 3NFDatabase products, indexes, partitions
PhysicalHow is it built on this platform?Engineers, DBAsTables, columns, types, constraints, indexes, partitions, storageBusiness ambiguity: everything must be decided
Reviewer's shortcut
Read each relationship aloud as a sentence in both directions: "Each policy term has exactly one named insured. Each party holds zero or many policy terms." If the business owner disagrees with either sentence, the model is wrong, however clean the tables look.

See the full conceptual, logical and physical models in Document 1: Data Model & ERD.

The discipline

2. Entities, relationships and cardinality

An entity is one kind of real thing the business must remember: a party, a policy term, a vehicle, a claim. Each entity has a stable key that identifies one instance for its whole life. A relationship connects two entities, and its cardinality says how many of each can participate.

ConceptRuleLedgerOne example
One entity per real thingIf two rows can describe the same real-world thing, you have a duplicate entity or a missing master.A driver is not its own entity. A driver is a Party listed on a policy (policy_driver), because the same person can also be the named insured and a claimant.
Stable keyUse a surrogate key that never changes, plus a unique business key that the business recognizes.policy_id (surrogate) plus (policy_number, term_number) unique.
Associative entityA many-to-many relationship becomes its own table, which usually carries facts of its own.policy_vehicle resolves Policy to Vehicle and holds usage, garaging ZIP and mileage.
Optional vs mandatoryThe inner symbol (circle or bar) says whether participation is optional.A claim may involve no insured vehicle (circle), but always belongs to one policy term (bar).
Every relationship states its cardinalityAn unlabelled line is an unanswered question.All 15 LedgerOne relationships are listed with both ends in Document 1.
Red flag in AI output
A customer_id column on the claim table with no relationship drawn, or a relationship line with no cardinality. Both mean the model has not decided who the claim is about, and reports will disagree about it.

3. Normalization vs denormalization

Normalize where data is written; denormalize only where it is read. Normalization stores each fact once, so an update happens in one place and cannot contradict itself. Denormalization copies facts onto wide rows so that reads are simple and fast. Both are correct, in different layers.

Normal formPlain-English testViolation and fix
1NFEvery column holds one value; no repeating groups.vehicle_1_vin, vehicle_2_vin, vehicle_3_vin on policy. Fix: policy_vehicle rows.
2NFEvery non-key column depends on the whole key.In policy_vehicle, model_year depends only on the vehicle. Fix: move it to vehicle.
3NFNo non-key column depends on another non-key column.product_name next to product_code on policy. Fix: reference code set; join for the name.
LayerShapeWhy
Core (system of record in the warehouse)3NFIntegrates three sources without contradiction; constraints catch bad data.
Marts (star schemas)Denormalized dimensions, narrow additive factsBusiness users and Power BI read simple joins; history via SCD2.
Feature views for AIOne wide row per entity per as-of dateModels need flat inputs; built from marts, never written to directly.
The one-wide-table trap
AI tools often propose a single policy_claims_payments table "for simplicity". It duplicates the policy on every claim and every payment, so a premium correction must be applied in thousands of rows, and totals double-count. Reject it as a system of record; it is acceptable only as a read-only view built from a normalized core.

4. OLTP vs OLAP, warehouse vs lake

OLTP systems run the business: they accept many small writes and must never lose one. OLAP systems explain the business: they answer large read questions across history. Running analytics on OLTP slows the business process; running the business on OLAP loses transactions. Keep them separate and move data between them deliberately.

OLTP (PAS-7, ClaimDesk, SettleFlow)OLAP (LedgerOne core and marts)
WorkloadThousands of small inserts and updates per minuteScans and aggregates over millions of rows
ShapeNormalized for write integrityNormalized core, denormalized marts
HistoryCurrent state, often overwrittenFull history: SCD2 dimensions, append-only facts
UsersAgents, adjusters, customersActuaries, finance, executives, AI features
StoreWhat it isUse it forDo not use it for
Data lakeFiles in object storage (Parquet, JSON, CSV), schema applied on readRaw landing, replay, cold archive, unstructured dataGoverned reporting: nothing enforces keys or meaning
Data warehouseTables with schemas, constraints and SQLIntegrated, governed, reportable dataRaw, unvalidated dumps
LakehouseLake storage with table formats (Delta, Iceberg) adding transactions and schemasLarge-scale analytics and ML on one copy of dataAn excuse to skip modeling: it still needs a core and marts
LedgerOne decision
Lake for the raw zone and cold archive; PostgreSQL 16 for core and marts, because the volume (under 5 TB hot) does not justify a separate engine and enforced constraints are worth more here than scale. Recorded with the trigger to revisit: hot data above 15 TB or query p95 above 10 seconds.

5. ETL vs ELT and pipelines

ETL transforms data before loading it into the warehouse. ELT loads raw data first and transforms it inside the warehouse with SQL. ELT is the modern default because the raw copy lets you replay and re-derive; ETL is still right when data must be changed before it lands (for example, tokenizing a license number so the clear value never touches disk).

ETLELT
Transform happensIn a pipeline engine, before loadIn the warehouse, after load
Raw copy keptOften notYes, in the raw zone
StrengthSensitive data never lands untransformedReplayable, auditable, SQL-visible logic

The idempotency rule

A pipeline is idempotent when running it twice for the same input produces the same end state. This is non-negotiable: pipelines are retried by schedulers, re-run by on-call engineers, and replayed after fixes. Each of the following patterns makes a stage safe to re-run.

PatternHow it worksWhere LedgerOne uses it
Idempotency key on the runA unique (pipeline, source, business_date) row; a re-run finds and reuses itaudit.load_batch, every stage
MERGE on business keysInsert if new, update if changed, never blind INSERTS4 core loads (policy_number + term_number, payment_reference)
Change detection by hashUpdate only when the business-column hash differsrecord_hash on every core table
Partition swapRebuild a whole date partition and swap it in atomicallyS6 fact builds
Stable surrogate keysA key is assigned once, at first insert; re-runs match on the business key and never mint a new oneEvery core table; party_id via party_source_xref

6. Data quality and lineage

Data quality is whether data is fit for its use, measured by rules with thresholds. Lineage is the traceable path from a reported number back to the source fields it came from. Together they let you answer the executive question "Can I trust this number?" with evidence rather than reassurance.

DimensionQuestionExample rule
ValidityIs the value allowed?VIN is 17 characters with a valid check digit.
CompletenessIs required data present?Every policy term resolves to a golden party.
UniquenessIs each real thing recorded once?One payment per gateway reference, even if it arrived by webhook and by file.
ConsistencyDo related values agree?Term premium equals the sum of its coverage premiums.
TimelinessIs it here when needed?Marts published by 06:00 CT.
SeverityGate actionUse for
BlockFail the batch; nothing is publishedErrors that would make every downstream number wrong (duplicates, control-total mismatch)
QuarantineDivert the bad rows; load the restRow-level errors that a steward can fix
WarnLoad, flag, and track the rateCosmetic or enrichable issues
Ask for the threshold and the owner
A rule without a threshold cannot fail, and a rule without an owner will not be fixed. Reject any quality plan that lists checks without both.

7. Master data and the data dictionary

Master data is the shared, slowly changing data that many processes refer to: customers, vehicles, products, locations. Reference data is the controlled code sets that classify it: states, coverage codes, causes of loss. Mastering produces a golden record: one trusted version of each real thing, assembled from all the sources that know it.

Mastering stepWhat it decides
MatchWhich source records describe the same real thing (rules M1 to M4, scored 0 to 1)
MergeWhether to link automatically (score at least 0.95), queue for a steward (0.80 to 0.95) or create a new record
SurviveWhich source wins each attribute (for example, PAS-7 wins name; most recent verified wins email)
CrosswalkKeeps every source key pointing to the golden key, so nothing is orphaned and merges can be undone

The data dictionary is the contract for every field: its name, type, meaning, source, whether it may be null, and whether it is personal data. A field that is not in the dictionary does not exist as far as governance is concerned. LedgerOne's dictionary covers 252 columns across 23 tables; see Document 2.

8. Governance, PII and retention

Governance decides who may see and change which data, how sensitive each field is, how long it is kept, and how it is destroyed. It is designed into the model, not added later: classification is a column in the dictionary, masking is a view in the physical design, and retention is a partition scheme.

ClassMeaningLedgerOne examplesDefault control
PublicApproved for anyone, including outside the company.Code descriptions, calendar, published rate filingsNone
InternalBusiness data with no personal identifiers.Policy numbers, premiums, claim amounts, codesEmployees with a business need; logged
ConfidentialPersonal data that identifies or contacts a person.Name, email, phone, street, ZIP, VIN, garaging ZIP, last 4Role-based; masked by default outside core; access reviewed quarterly
RestrictedPersonal data whose exposure causes legal or financial harm.Birth date, license token, payment instrument token, claim free textNamed individuals only; break-glass with ticket; every read logged
PrincipleIn practice
MinimizeDo not bring data you do not need. LedgerOne excludes SSN, card numbers, bank account numbers and clear license numbers entirely.
Tokenize before landingRestricted identifiers are replaced by vault tokens in S0, so the clear value never reaches the warehouse disk.
De-identify for analyticsMarts carry age bands and 3-digit ZIPs, not birth dates and full addresses.
Least privilege by roleAccess is granted to roles, against classes, through views; never to individuals on base tables.
Retention is a date, not a feelingEach data category has a period, a trigger event, and a deletion method. Legal hold overrides deletion.
Red flag in AI output
A governance policy that says "PII will be protected appropriately" with no field list, no class per field, no retention period and no deletion method. That is an intention, not a policy.
The discipline

9. Schema evolution

Schemas change: sources add fields, regulators require new attributes, the business renames things. Schema evolution is changing a schema without breaking the pipelines, reports and models that depend on it. The tool is to classify each change and apply the matching pattern.

ChangeBreaking?Pattern
Add a nullable columnNoAdd it; backfill if needed; document in the dictionary
Add a NOT NULL columnYes for writersAdd nullable, backfill, then add the constraint
Rename a columnYes for readersExpand and contract: add new, dual-write, migrate readers, drop old
Change a type (widen)Usually noVARCHAR(20) to VARCHAR(40) is safe; test downstream lengths
Change a type (narrow) or meaningYesNew column with a new name; never silently repurpose
Source drops a fieldYesS1 contract check fails the batch before load; the source owner is notified
Data contracts
Each LedgerOne source has a contract: the fields, types and row-count bounds it promises. S1 checks every batch against it. A breaking source change becomes a failed batch with a clear message, not a quietly wrong dashboard.

Why constraints and quality rules both exist
Quality rules (LO-DA-05) catch bad rows before they reach core and route them to quarantine with a reason. Constraints are the last line of defense if a pipeline bug skips a rule: the database refuses the row and the batch fails loudly instead of loading silently wrong data. If this schema is ported to a warehouse that does not enforce constraints (for example Snowflake), the constraints become documentation and every one of them must have a matching blocking quality rule.

4. Masked views

-- Masked views: Confidential fields masked, Restricted fields removed.
CREATE VIEW core_v.party AS
SELECT party_id, party_type_code,
       left(given_name, 1) || '***'                     AS given_name_masked,
       left(family_name, 1) || '***'                    AS family_name_masked,
       organization_name,
       regexp_replace(email_address, '^(.).*@', '\1***@') AS email_masked,
       '***-***-' || right(phone_number, 4)             AS phone_masked,
       city_name, state_code, left(postal_code, 3)      AS postal_code_3,
       is_active, updated_at
FROM core.party;                         -- birth_date and address_line_1 are not exposed

CREATE VIEW core_v.payment AS
SELECT payment_id, payment_reference, direction_code, policy_id, party_id,
       method_code, amount, currency_code, status_code, initiated_at, settled_at,
       instrument_last4                  -- instrument_token is not exposed
FROM core.payment;

CREATE VIEW core_v.claim AS
SELECT claim_id, claim_number, policy_id, policy_vehicle_id, loss_date, reported_date,
       loss_cause_code, loss_state_code, fault_code, status_code, closed_date
FROM core.claim;                         -- loss_description (Restricted) is not exposed

Glossary

TermMeaning
3NFThird normal form: every non-key column depends on the key, the whole key, and nothing but the key.
Business keyThe identifier the business uses (policy number). Unique, but may change format; paired with a surrogate key.
CardinalityHow many instances of one entity relate to one instance of another (one, zero-or-one, one-or-many, zero-or-many).
CDCChange data capture: reading inserts, updates and deletes from a source log instead of full extracts.
Conformed dimensionA dimension shared with the same keys and meaning by several fact tables.
CrosswalkA table mapping each source key to the golden key (party_source_xref).
Data contractA source's promise of fields, types and volumes, checked on every batch.
Degenerate dimensionA dimension value stored on the fact with no dimension table (claim_number).
Fact tableMeasures at a declared grain, with foreign keys to dimensions.
Golden recordThe single trusted version of a master entity, assembled by match and survivorship.
GrainExactly what one row of a table represents.
IdempotentSafe to run more than once: same input, same end state, no duplicates.
Legal holdA flag that suspends deletion because data may be needed for litigation or investigation.
LineageThe documented path from a reported value back to its source fields and transformations.
MERGEA SQL statement that inserts new rows and updates changed rows in one atomic step.
QuarantineHolding area for rows that failed a quality rule, kept for steward triage.
SCD2Slowly changing dimension type 2: a new row per change, with validity dates, preserving history.
Surrogate keyA meaningless, stable, system-generated identifier.
SurvivorshipRules choosing which source supplies each attribute of a golden record.
TokenizationReplacing a sensitive value with a reference token held in a secure vault.
